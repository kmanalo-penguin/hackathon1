# Farkle — Rules & Reference


## A Brief History

Farkle is a folk dice game with murky but likely medieval European origins. It was played in taverns and inns
as a simple game of nerve and luck: how long do you keep pressing your fortune before it runs out?

The core tension — bank what you have, or risk it all for more — has made it a staple of game nights for
centuries.


## The Goal

Be the first player to reach **2,000 points**.


## What You Need

- **6 six-sided dice**
- Something to keep score
- Nerves


## Turn Structure

On your turn:

1. **Roll all 6 dice.**
2. **Set aside at least one scoring die or combination.** You must score something — if nothing scores, you
   bust and your turn ends.
3. **Choose your fate:**
   - **Bank it** — add your turn total to your running score and end your turn, OR
   - **Push your luck** — roll the remaining dice, hoping to score more.
4. If you score with all 6 dice without busting, you may pick up all 6 and roll again, carrying your
   accumulated turn points with you. Be warned — you still risk it all if you bust on that roll.
5. Repeat until you bank or bust.


## Bust!

If you roll and **none of the dice score**, you bust.

You lose **all points accumulated during that turn**. Control passes to the next player.


## Scoring

**Singles** — only 1s and 5s score on their own:

| Die | Points |
|---|---|
| Single **1** | 100 |
| Single **5** | 50 |

**Three of a kind** — multiply the die face by 100. Exception: three 1s = 1,000.

| Combination | Points |
|---|---|
| Three **1s** | 1,000 |
| Three **2s** | 200 (2 x 100) |
| Three **3s** | 300 (3 x 100) |
| Three **4s** | 400 (4 x 100) |
| Three **5s** | 500 (5 x 100) |
| Three **6s** | 600 (6 x 100) |

**Sets:**

| Combination | Points |
|---|---|
| **Four of a kind** (any) | 1,000 |
| **Five of a kind** (any) | 2,000 |
| **Six of a kind** (any) | 3,000 |
| **Three pair** (e.g., 2-2-4-4-6-6) | 1,500 |
| **Run** (1-2-3-4-5-6, all 6 dice) | 2,500 |

**Key rules for scoring:**

- You may only score combinations from a **single roll**. You cannot combine dice across multiple rolls to
  form three of a kind.
- Each die can only be used in **one** combination per roll.
- A single 1 or single 5 that is part of a three-of-a-kind does **not** also score its individual value.
- Leftover singles that don't form combinations (except 1s and 5s) are worth nothing.


## Quick Examples

**Roll: 1, 1, 1, 5, 3, 4**
- Three 1s = 1,000
- Single 5 = 50
- 3 and 4 = nothing
- **Turn total if banked: 1,050**

**Roll: 2, 3, 4, 6, 6, 2**
- Nothing scores. **Bust!** Turn ends, points lost.

**Roll: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6**
- Run = 2,500
- **Turn total if banked: 2,500** (instant win!)

**Roll: 3, 3, 5, 5, 1, 1**
- Three pair = 1,500
- All 6 dice scored — pick up all 6 and roll again if you dare.

**Roll: 5, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5**
- Six 5s = 3,000
- All 6 dice scored — pick up all 6 and roll again.


## Strategy Notes

There's no objectively correct strategy — that's part of the game. But here are the key decisions players face:

- **When to bank:** A small guaranteed score is always better than nothing. Don't chase a big hand if you're
  close to winning.
- **All-six pressure:** If all your dice scored, rolling all 6 again feels exciting — but you're putting your
  entire turn total on the line. One bad roll wipes it out.
- **Watch the count:** Rolling 1 or 2 remaining dice is risky — only 1s and 5s save you. Think before you
  leave a small number of dice on the table.
- **Know your opponent:** If the other player is far ahead, you need to take risks. If you're ahead, bank early.
